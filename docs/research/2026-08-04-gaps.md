### Verdict on the SCIENCE.md characterisation

The direction of the claim survives, and is in fact *stronger* than "almost all"
— Barrick & Mount found C valid for **all five** occupational groups and **all
three** criterion types, with no exception. But three things are wrong or
missing:

1. "is the most replicated finding in applied personality psychology" is an
   uncited superlative about the literature, not something the 1991 paper
   claims. A single 1991 meta-analysis is not a "finding" that is "replicated".
2. No magnitude is given. ρ ≈ .22 is a modest effect and the document should
   say so, because the rest of SCIENCE.md is careful about overreach.
3. "Bell (2007) confirms the effect at team level" is true only in field
   settings. See W4 below: Bell's overall team-level C estimate is ρ = .11
   (field ρ = .30, lab ρ = .04).

### Exact replacement text for lines 109–113

```
**Conscientiousness (Discipline)** is directional upward. Barrick & Mount (1991)
meta-analysed 162 samples drawn from 117 studies (total N = 23,994) and found
Conscientiousness to be a valid predictor
for all five occupational groups they examined (professionals, police, managers,
sales, skilled/semi-skilled) and for all three criterion types (job proficiency,
training proficiency, personnel data). It was the only one of the five
dimensions to hold across every cell: estimated true score correlations ranged
from .20 to .23 by occupation and .20 to .23 by criterion, mean ρ = .22. The
effect is consistent but modest, and the authors themselves note the validities
are underestimates of construct-level validity because composite scores were not
available. Bell (2007) finds the same direction at team level, though only in
field settings (team mean Conscientiousness ρ = .30 in the field, ρ = .04 in the
lab, ρ = .11 overall). No cited source reports a benefit from low-C members as a
counterweight.
```

Reference entry at lines 608–610 is already correct as written. Metadata
re-confirmed against Crossref: *Personnel Psychology*, 44(1), 1–26,
Murray R. Barrick and Michael K. Mount, 1991.

## W4 / lines 115–118. Bell (2007) and Neuroticism

The JAP article is based in part on Bell's Texas A&M dissertation (Bell, S. T.,
2004, *Setting the stage for effective teams: a meta-analysis of team design
variables and team effectiveness*, hdl:1969.1/1110, supervised by Winfred
Arthur Jr.). OpenAlex records it as green OA with a direct bitstream URL at
`oaktrust.library.tamu.edu`. **Blocked by a Cloudflare bot challenge that I did
not attempt to solve**, from curl, from WebFetch, and from a real browser
session that sat on "Performing security verification" for 16 s without
clearing. This is the only route I abandoned on policy rather than on
availability, and it is the one a human with a browser could finish in a minute.

Its OpenAlex abstract is readable and is consistent with everything below:

> "For lab studies (intellectual teams), team general mental ability (GMA) and
> task-relevant expertise were strong predictors of team performance, while team
> personality variables were unrelated to team performance. In field studies
> (physical teams), team agreeableness and conscientiousness had stronger
> relationships with team performance than team GMA and team task-relevant
> expertise."

**The route that worked** is the cited-by chain. Kozlowski & Bell (2013), a
Wiley *Handbook of Psychology* chapter, reproduces Bell's per-factor,
per-setting corrected correlations in full. Note that the Bell of Kozlowski &
Bell is **Bradford S. Bell of Cornell**, a different person from Suzanne T.
Bell, so this is an independent reading of her tables and not self-report. The
author manuscript is openly posted by Cornell and I read it:

```
Kozlowski, S. W. J., & Bell, B. S. (2013). Work groups and teams in
organizations: Review update. In N. Schmitt & S. Highhouse (Eds.), *Handbook of
psychology: Vol. 12. Industrial and organizational psychology* (2nd ed.,
pp. 412–469). Wiley.
Author manuscript: https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/42df91e0-eb80-406b-8647-6c5adb353ed5/content
```

Page 15 of that manuscript, verbatim:

> "A meta-analysis by Bell (2007) revealed that the overall effects of team
> personality composition on team effectiveness were quite modest
> (conscientiousness, ρ = .11; agreeableness, ρ = .12; extraversion, ρ = .09;
> emotional stability, ρ = .04; openness to experience, ρ = .05). However, she
> also found that study setting was a strong moderator of the team personality
> composition and team performance relationships. The relationships were
> stronger in field settings (conscientiousness, ρ = .30; agreeableness, ρ = .31;
> extraversion, ρ = .15; emotional stability, ρ = .06; openness to experience,
> ρ = .00) compared to lab settings (conscientiousness, ρ = .04; agreeableness,
> ρ = .03; extraversion, ρ = .06; emotional stability, ρ = .03; openness to
> experience, ρ = .20). Bell also examined team composition operationalization
> (e.g., mean, maximum, minimum, heterogeneity, other) as a moderating factor and
> found that the strongest effects were observed in field settings when team
> personality composition was operationalized as the team mean, with the
> exception of agreeableness which had an equally strong effect when
> operationalized as the team minimum."

### Bell (2007) per factor, per operationalisation, per setting, as reported

Corrected population correlations ρ with team performance:

| Factor | Overall | Field | Lab |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conscientiousness | .11 | **.30** | .04 |
| Agreeableness | .12 | **.31** | .03 |
| Extraversion | .09 | .15 | .06 |
| Emotional stability | **.04** | **.06** | **.03** |
| Openness to experience | .05 | .00 (contested, see Bonus) | .20 (contested) |

Operationalisation: strongest effects in field settings at the **team mean** for
every factor **except Agreeableness**, which was equally strong at the **team
minimum**. This is the source of the "team minimum agreeableness" phrase in
Bell's abstract, and it is the only minimum-score result among the personality
factors.

Corroborating, independently readable: Mathieu, Gallagher, Domingo & Klock
(2019), *Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational
Behavior*, p. 25:

> "Bell (2007) demonstrated that in the field, despite individual-level
> attributes, a team's average conscientiousness, agreeableness, and
> extraversion, positively predicted performance."

Three factors named. Not emotional stability. Not openness.

Two further second-hand values I found in the cited-by chain and am **not**
treating as established, because I could not read their sources in full: team
minimum conscientiousness ρ = .27 and team minimum agreeableness ρ = .37 against
team maximum ρ = .14 for both; and team GMA ρ = .27 mean / .34 minimum / .27
maximum. Recorded here only so the next reader does not have to re-derive them.

### Verdict

The current sentence is wrong on three counts, and the audit was right to flag
it but understated the case:

1. **Magnitude.** Emotional stability is Bell's *weakest* personality predictor
   in every cell: ρ = .04 overall, .06 field, .03 lab. There is no "consistent
   negative effect".
2. **Sign convention.** Bell scores emotional stability, not neuroticism. A
   positive ρ of .04 for emotional stability implies roughly −.04 for
   neuroticism, which is indistinguishable from zero.
3. **Criterion.** Bell's criterion throughout is team *performance*. Cohesion is
   not a criterion in the paper. The word "cohesion" in SCIENCE.md has no source.

### Exact replacement text for lines 115–118

```
**Neuroticism (Depth)** is directional downward, but weakly. In Bell's (2007)
meta-analysis, emotional stability is the weakest of the five personality
factors at team level in every setting: corrected ρ = .04 overall, .06 in field
studies and .03 in lab studies, against .30 and .31 for team mean
Conscientiousness and Agreeableness in the field. Bell's criterion is team
performance; she does not study cohesion. Cèrcol therefore treats Depth as
directional downward on the general trait literature rather than on a team-level
finding, and no cited source establishes a team role that requires high N as a
desirable characteristic in itself.
```

## The audit's own W5 replacement text is now contested on Openness

This is the item that turned out worse than the audit thought, and it is in text
that has **already been applied** to SCIENCE.md. Line 36 currently reads:

> "The selection of Bond (A) and Vision (O) draws on Bell (2007), which found
> team minimum agreeableness and team mean openness to experience among the
> strongest composition predictors of team performance in field studies..."

That sentence tracks Bell's abstract, which does say it, verbatim from PubMed
(PMID 17484544, read in full):

> "In contrast, team minimum agreeableness and team mean conscientiousness,
> openness to experience, collectivism, and preference for teamwork emerged as
> strong predictors of team performance in field studies."

But **two independently readable secondary sources contradict the Openness half
of it**:

- Kozlowski & Bell (2013) report Bell's Openness as **ρ = .00 in field settings
  and ρ = .20 in lab settings** — exactly inverted from what the abstract
  implies, and also in tension with the abstract's own "Only negligible effects
  were observed in lab settings for the personality factor and team performance
  relationships".
- Mathieu et al. (2019) list Bell's field predictors as "a team's average
  conscientiousness, agreeableness, and extraversion". Openness is not among
  them.

One of these is wrong and I cannot tell which without Bell's tables. Either
Kozlowski & Bell transposed the lab and field columns for Openness, or the
abstract's Openness claim rests on a cell with very few studies that later
readers declined to repeat. Both readings are bad for SCIENCE.md, because Vision
(O) is one of the three balance dimensions and half the definition of six of the
twelve roles — the same structural exposure the post-mortem already identified
for Presence (E).

**Recommendation.** Until Bell's tables are read, line 36 should not present
team mean Openness as an established field predictor. Suggested narrowing:

```
The selection of Bond (A) draws on Bell (2007), which found team minimum
agreeableness among the strongest composition predictors of team performance in
field studies (team mean A rho = .31, with the team minimum equally strong), and
on Neuman & Wright (1999), which found Agreeableness predictive of team
performance and interpersonal skills in 79 four-person work teams. Bell's
abstract also lists team mean openness to experience as a field predictor, but
two later reviews reading her tables report Openness at or near zero in field
settings (Kozlowski & Bell 2013; Mathieu et al. 2019), so Cèrcol does not treat
Vision (O) as carried over from that finding. Neither study examined
Extraversion; the inclusion of Presence (P) as a balance dimension is a design
decision. Neither study tested a bipolar balance effect: both report
directional, mean- or minimum-level relationships, so the claim that these
dimensions require representation at both poles is Cèrcol's hypothesis, not a
result imported from the literature.
```

This makes Vision (O) a second design decision alongside Presence (E), leaving
Bond (A) as the only balance dimension with clean support in the cited
literature. That is a substantive downgrade and should be reflected wherever
line 78 makes the same claim.

## Barrick & Mount contains a directly relevant negative result for Vision

Openness in Barrick & Mount is essentially null at the individual level for job
proficiency (ρ = −.03, *N* = 9,454, *k* = 55) and near zero for four of five
occupational groups, its **only** substantial validity being for training
proficiency (ρ = .25). If SCIENCE.md wants an individual-level anchor for Vision
it will not find one there, and the document should not imply otherwise.

## Extraversion has a stronger individual-level anchor than the document uses

Also from Barrick & Mount: Extraversion ρ = .18 for managers and .15 for sales
(the two interpersonally loaded occupations, as they hypothesised), and ρ = .26
for training proficiency pooled across occupations. Given that Presence (E) is
currently documented as a bare design decision with no cited support, these are
the closest published anchors that exist in the document's own reference list.
They are individual-level and occupation-specific, so they do not rescue the
team-level balance claim, but they are better than nothing and they are already
cited.
