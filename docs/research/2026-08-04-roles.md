# What grounds the twelve roles

Research report on the provenance and defensibility of Cèrcol's 12-role
system: the AB5C claim, the Discipline and Depth offsets, the evidence base
for team-role typologies, and the statistics of nearest-centroid
classification of continuous traits.

Everything cited below was opened and read. Routes are given for each source
so a reader can repeat them. Section 6 reports a simulation run against
Cèrcol's own centroid file; the script is at
`scratchpad/papers/stability.py` and can be rerun.

---

## Summary of the verdict

The AB5C claim is **half real and stated in a way that overclaims**.

Real: the three planes Cèrcol uses (Presence × Bond, Presence × Vision,
Bond × Vision) are genuinely three of the AB5C's ten circumplex planes, and
the idea of locating a construct as a blend of two of the five factors is
exactly what Hofstee, De Raad & Goldberg (1992) built.

Not real: no Cèrcol role corresponds to an AB5C facet. Every role sits at
45° in its plane, which in the AB5C's 30° segmentation is precisely the
boundary between two adjacent facets. Two of the six facet pairs Cèrcol
occupies are among the eleven the paper reports as *not* well defined, and
one of the twelve cells (Bear's) is empty in the published table. The AB5C
is a taxonomy of trait *adjectives*, not of people; it makes no claim that
persons cluster anywhere. And the role descriptions do not track the AB5C
content at those coordinates: at Bear's location the published adjectives
are "simple, dependent, servile".

The twelve roles are an **authored design layer** placed on a measured
profile. That is a legitimate thing to build. It is not what the current
documentation says it is.

## 1. The AB5C claim

### Current text

`SCIENCE.md`, "Scientific foundation":

> Goldberg 1992), a model that organises personality as intersections of
> OCEAN factor pairs. **Each AB5C facet has a known, measured OCEAN profile
> published in the public domain (ipip.ori.org).**

`SCIENCE.md`, Step 2:

> We define roles as intersections of these three dimensions at both poles,
> **following the AB5C circumplex structure (Hofstee et al. 1992)**. […]
> The AB5C structure is the published scientific framework [em dash in
> source] we are applying it, not inventing it.

`src/utils/role-scoring.js` line 14 and `SCIENCE.md` line 155:

> (Bell 2007)

### The source

Hofstee, W. K. B., De Raad, B., & Goldberg, L. R. (1992). Integration of the
Big Five and circumplex approaches to trait structure. *Journal of
Personality and Social Psychology, 63*(1), 146–163.
https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.63.1.146

**Route:** the author copy is open at the Oregon Research Institute,
https://projects.ori.org/lrg/PDFs_papers/Bigfive-circum-jpsp92.pdf
(Goldberg's own repository). No paywall. Full 18-page article read, including
the complete Table 1 (pp. 156–160) and the Discussion (pp. 161–162).

### What the paper actually builds

Abstract, p. 146:

> "To integrate the 5-dimensional simple-structure and circumplex models of
> personality, the Abridged Big Five Dimensional Circumplex (AB5C) taxonomy
> of personality traits was developed, consisting of the 10 circumplexes
> that can be formed by pitting each of the Big Five factors against one
> another. The model maps facets of the Big Five dimensions as blends of 2
> factors. An application to data consisting of 636 self-ratings and peer
> ratings on 540 personality trait adjectives yielded 34 well-defined facets
> out of a possible 45."

The mechanics, p. 147:

> "Further economy is achieved by partitioning the circumplex planes into a
> limited number of segments, rather than retaining the exact angular
> positions of the trait variables. […] Here, we partition the circumplex
> planes into segments of 30° each, inserting additional factors at angles
> of 30° and 60° with each of the basic factors."

and:

> "Only the two highest loadings of a variable are considered. If the primary
> loading is at least 3.73 as large as the secondary loading, the variable is
> assigned to the pure-factor segment associated with the primary loading.
> For example, *extraverted*, with its highest loading of .65 on Factor I and
> its highest secondary loading of .09 on Factor II, would be assigned to the
> I+I+ segment."

So an AB5C facet is an **ordered** pair: a primary factor pole and a
secondary factor pole. `I+II+` (primary Extraversion, secondary
Agreeableness) and `II+I+` (primary Agreeableness, secondary Extraversion)
are *different facets*, occupying different 30° segments of the same plane.
Ninety unipolar facets, grouped into 45 bipolar ones.

### The geometric problem

The 12 segments of a plane have bisectrices at 0°, 30°, 60°, 90° and so on.
In the I × II plane: `I+I+` at 0°, `I+II+` at 30°, `II+I+` at 60°, `II+II+`
at 90°. Segment boundaries therefore fall at 15°, 45°, 75° …

Every Cèrcol role is defined by equal ±1.0 z-scores on its two dimensions
and 0.0 on the third balance dimension. That is **exactly 45°**: precisely
the boundary between the two segments, the one angle in the plane at which
the AB5C assignment is undefined.

A role at (Presence +1.0, Bond +1.0) is therefore not "the AB5C facet for
P+B+". There is no such facet. It straddles two.

### The mapping, built

Cèrcol's dimensions in Goldberg's factor numbering as used in the paper:
Presence = Factor I (Extraversion), Bond = Factor II (Agreeableness),
Discipline = Factor III (Conscientiousness), Vision = Factor V
(Intellect/Openness). **Factor IV in this paper is Emotional Stability, not
Neuroticism**, so Cèrcol's Depth maps to IV−. Neither SCIENCE.md nor
PRODUCT.md states this, and it is a live source of error for anyone reading
Table 1 against the centroid table.

Cèrcol uses three of the ten planes: I × II, I × V, II × V. It uses none of
I × III, I × IV, II × III, II × IV, III × IV, III × V, IV × V.

### Which of these are among the 34 well-defined facets

The paper names them, p. 148:

> "Of the nine bipolar facets of Factor I, eight are well-defined:
> talkative–silent, sociable–unsociable, dominant–submissive,
> competitive–uncompetitive, boisterous–restrained, courageous–cowardly,
> explosive–sedate, and adventurous–unadventurous. **The only facet that is
> insufficiently defined is I+V− versus I−V+.**"

I+V− versus I−V+ is **Falcon and Octopus**. The single bipolar facet of
Factor I that the AB5C data could not define is the one Cèrcol places two of
its twelve roles on.

> "Factor II also has seven well-defined facets: sympathetic–unsympathetic,
> friendly–unfriendly, agreeable–rough, considerate–inconsiderate,
> generous–selfish, affectionate–unaffectionate, and tactful–tactless, the
> latter being well-defined only at one pole."

II+V− versus II−V+ is absent from that list. That is **Bear and Fox**. And
tactful–tactless, "well-defined only at one pole", is **Bee and Badger**,
the well-defined pole being Badger's (nine terms) not Bee's (two).

> "Factor V has seven well-defined facets, two of which, however, are
> well-defined at only one pole: creative–uncreative,
> inquisitive–uninquisitive, introspective, deep–shallow,
> individualistic–dependent, perceptive–unobservant, and intellectual."

So the six bipolar facet pairs Cèrcol's twelve roles occupy break down as:
four well-defined at both poles (Dolphin/Owl, Wolf/Elephant, Eagle/Tortoise,
plus Badger's pole of Bee/Badger), and two that the paper's own results
report as under-defined or empty (Falcon/Octopus, Bear/Fox).

### The AB5C is a taxonomy of adjectives, not of persons

This is the load-bearing point and the documentation does not acknowledge it.
The AB5C classifies the 540 trait terms of the English lexicon by where they
fall in five-factor space. Discussion, p. 162:

> "The manner in which traits are assigned to facets in the models is based
> entirely on the common denominator of laypersons' cognitive maps of
> personality."

The paper never asserts that people cluster at facet locations, never
estimates how many people occupy any facet, and never proposes facets as
types. The claim in SCIENCE.md that "each AB5C facet has a known, measured
OCEAN profile" is true only in the sense that each facet has a known angular
position derived from adjective loadings. That is not a person-level profile
and it is not a norm.

### The authors explicitly decline the move Cèrcol makes

Section "Applications of the Solution", p. 161, where they place Holland's six
vocational **types** into AB5C space:

> "The analysis shows that all trait dimensions except IV are clearly
> represented in this typology, which therefore cannot be meaningfully
> represented in a plane. […] **We do not wish to imply that types should fit
> into one of the boxes of the AB5C taxonomy.** On the contrary, types such
> as Analytical (V+III+) and Introspective (V+I−), characterized by a blend
> of three or more factors, may be found to be interesting and useful."

And on the model's own status, Discussion p. 161:

> "We do not presume that the AB5C model is definitive or comprehensive.
> Rather, it is demonstrably procrustean in that it discards any variance
> that is not covered by the first five principal components, and it does not
> deal adequately with those few variables that load highly on more than two
> of these factors."

### The role descriptions do not follow from the AB5C content

If the roles were derived from the AB5C, the role copy would reflect the
adjectives at those coordinates. It does not.

- **Bear** (B+ V−). AB5C content: an empty cell on one side, and "simple,
  dependent, servile" on the other. PRODUCT.md copy: "You do not change under
  pressure. The team knows you are there, and that knowing frees them to take
  risks."
- **Badger** (B− V−). AB5C content: uncharitable, ruthless, coarse,
  narrow-minded, callous, tactless, curt, bigoted, vindictive, shallow, terse.
  PRODUCT.md copy: "You are not interested in what should work. You are
  interested in what works."
- **Falcon** (P+ V−). AB5C content: verbose, unscrupulous, pompous.
  PRODUCT.md copy: "It is not vision, it is timing. You know exactly when to
  move."
- **Octopus** (P− V+). AB5C content: inner-directed, introspective,
  meditative, contemplating, self-examining. PRODUCT.md copy: "The team's
  best ideas often passed through you first, in silence." (This one is close.)

The valence reversals on Bear, Badger and Falcon are not translation into
warm product voice; they are different content. That is the clearest evidence
that the descriptions were authored and the AB5C was cited afterwards.

## 2. The offsets on Discipline and Depth

### Current text

`SCIENCE.md` and both scoring files carry twelve five-tuples in which
Discipline takes values in {−0.8, −0.3, 0.0, +0.5, +0.8, +1.0} and Depth in
{−0.8, −0.5, −0.3, 0.0, +0.3}, attributed to "AB5C (Hofstee et al. 1992) +
OCEAN team composition literature (Bell 2007)".

### What I found

**There is no published basis for these magnitudes. They are authored.**
Saying so plainly is the finding.

Three lines of evidence.

**(a) The AB5C contains no z-scores.** Table 1 of Hofstee et al. reports
projection lengths of adjectives onto segment bisectrices (h = a₁cos30° +
a₂cos60°, p. 147), ranging .20 to .77. These are factor-loading projections
of *words*, not standardised scores of *persons*, and there is no
transformation that turns a projection of .62 for "responsible" into a
centroid offset of +0.8 on Discipline. No table in the paper has the shape of
the Cèrcol centroid table.

**(b) Bell (2007) reports team-level relationships, not role coordinates.**

Bell, S. T. (2007). Deep-level composition variables as predictors of team
performance: A meta-analysis. *Journal of Applied Psychology, 92*(3),
595–615. https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.92.3.595

**Route:** abstract retrieved verbatim from OpenAlex
(`api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1037/0021-9010.92.3.595`); article itself
paywalled at APA PsycNet.

> "In lab settings, team minimum and maximum general mental ability and team
> mean emotional intelligence were related to team performance. Only
> negligible effects were observed in lab settings for the personality factor
> and team performance relationships […] In contrast, team minimum
> agreeableness and team mean conscientiousness, openness to experience,
> collectivism, and preference for teamwork emerged as strong predictors of
> team performance in field studies."

These are aggregate operationalisations (team minimum, team mean) correlated
with team outcome. There is no per-person, per-role quantity in the paper at
all. Bell cannot yield a Discipline offset of +0.8 for Owl and +0.5 for Wolf,
because Bell contains nothing that distinguishes an Owl from a Wolf.

**(c) The git history shows the values arriving without derivation.** All
twelve tuples were introduced whole in commit `98363d8d3`, "chore: complete
Phase 8.3, 12-role animal system", whose message reads "Replace 9-role
system (R0 Opal–R8 Comet) with 12-role animal system (R01 Dolphin–R12
Badger) grounded in AB5C circumplex and Bell 2007." No working, no
derivation, no intermediate values, and the system they replaced had a
different number of roles with different names. The offsets were chosen, not
computed.

### One thing that *is* citable, and is not currently cited

The decision to treat Depth as a modulator rather than a balance dimension
has an AB5C basis that SCIENCE.md does not use. Hofstee et al., p. 148:

> "Factor IV, which functions predominantly as a modifier of other factors in
> the AB5C model, has only five well-defined facets, namely,
> unenvious–jealous, unselfconscious–insecure, unexcitable–excitable, and
> unemotional–emotional."

That is direct support for "Depth modulates how each role is expressed rather
than defining it". It is **not** support for the same treatment of
Discipline: Factor III has seven well-defined facets in the same analysis, the
same count as Factors II and V, and shows no modifier behaviour. SCIENCE.md's
current argument for excluding Conscientiousness rests on Barrick & Mount
(1991) and Bell (2007) being directional, which is a different and weaker
argument, and one that does not explain why C then appears in every centroid
with a non-trivial offset.

### Recommended replacement text

In `SCIENCE.md`, under the centroid table:

> which poles. The Discipline and Depth entries are authored. No published

## 3. Do team-role typologies have psychometric support?

### The honest state of the evidence: no, and the Belbin literature is the
### reason to know it

All four abstracts below were retrieved verbatim from OpenAlex
(`api.openalex.org/works/doi:…`), which carries them where the publisher
elides them from Semantic Scholar and Crossref. Full texts are paywalled at
Wiley; I note where that limited me.

**Furnham, A., Steele, H., & Pendleton, D. (1993). A psychometric assessment
of the Belbin Team-Role Self-Perception Inventory. *Journal of Occupational
and Organizational Psychology, 66*(3), 245–257.**
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8325.1993.tb00535.x

> "This paper set out to examine the psychometric properties of the
> extensively used, but little tested, Belbin (1981) Team-Role
> Self-Perception Inventory which examines how people behave in teams. The
> original 56-item inventory was given to over 100 people from a variety of
> backgrounds in a non-ipsative Likert scaling form. **The alpha coefficients
> for the eight roles were not impressive and factor analysis did not provide
> clear evidence of the proposed structure.** The second experiment attempted
> a similar non-ipsative analysis with the more recent 70-item version of the
> questionnaire which has nine team roles. Again the alpha coefficients were
> modest and the factor analysis suggested a more simple solution than
> suggested. Team-role scores did not correlate significantly with a large
> number of demographic factors any more than may be expected by chance. A
> third experiment used the original scale with the original ipsative scoring
> system using full-time managers as subjects. **Again alpha levels were low,
> intercorrelations not as predicted and the factor structure unclear.**"

Three studies, two scoring formats, two inventory versions: the structure did
not appear in any of them.

**Broucek, W. G., & Randell, G. (1996). An assessment of the construct
validity of the Belbin Self-Perception Inventory and Observer's Assessment
from the perspective of the five-factor model. *Journal of Occupational and
Organizational Psychology, 69*(4), 389–405.**
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8325.1996.tb00625.x

> "A total of 152 managers (101 men, 51 women) completed the SPI and were
> assessed by four knowledgeable co-workers each (608 raters) using the OA.
> **Results showed that the convergent and discriminant construct validities
> of the two measures did not support the contention that they were parallel
> forms.** […] Though the ipsative, forced-choice format of the SPI was
> identified as a source of invalidity, results showed that **neither the
> SPI, either in ipsative or normative version, nor the OA evidenced
> convergent and discriminant construct validity relative to the NEO-PI-R.**"

This is the closest published analogue to Cèrcol's own design, a self-report
role instrument plus a peer instrument, evaluated against the five-factor
model. The self and peer instruments did not agree with each other, and
neither showed construct validity against the Big Five.

**Anderson, N., & Sleap, S. (2004). An evaluation of gender differences on
the Belbin Team Role Self-Perception Inventory. *Journal of Occupational and
Organizational Psychology, 77*(3), 429–437.**
https://doi.org/10.1348/0963179041752637

> "Based on a sample of 311 sales and customer service representatives (208
> females, 103 males) […] **Findings suggest significant differences between
> genders in response patterns favouring males on the team roles of Chair,
> Shaper, and Plant. Conversely, females scored significantly higher on the
> role of Team Worker.** Implications for potential adverse impact,
> Four-Fifths Rule differences, and the use of Belbin's questionnaire for
> selection, particularly into team leader positions, are raised."

Note the specific hazard: the leadership-flavoured roles skewed male, the
supportive role skewed female, in an instrument whose vendor markets it for
team composition.

**The most favourable review still finds weak discriminant validity.**
Aritzeta, A., Swailes, S., & Senior, B. (2007). Belbin's team role model:
Development, validity and applications for team building. *Journal of
Management Studies, 44*(1), 96–118.
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-6486.2007.00666.x

> "The psychometric properties of the Team Role Self-Perception Inventory
> used to assess a person's likely behaviour in a team are examined along
> with 43 empirical studies […] While the evidence is mixed, we conclude
> that, on balance, the model and its accompanying Inventory have adequate
> convergent validity. **However, strong associations between some team roles
> are observed, indicating weak discriminant validity among some scales in
> the Inventory.**"

**The vendor concedes the point about the published instrument.** Belbin
Associates, *Method, Reliability & Validity, Statistics & Research: A
Comprehensive Review* (2014), p. 6:
https://www.belbin.com/media/1158/belbin-uk-2014-a-comprehensive-review.pdf

> "The self-scoring questionnaire is now obsolete and is not a reliable way
> of determining Team Roles."

That is the instrument that every independent study above tested, and the
instrument in the book that made the model famous.

### Does the criticism apply to Cèrcol's twelve roles?

Partly, and the parts that differ are worth stating precisely, because
Cèrcol is better than Belbin on two axes and no better on the third.

**Better: the measurement.** Belbin's problems begin at the item level,
ipsative scoring, low alphas, absent factor structure. Cèrcol's measurement
is IPIP-NEO, with published domain alphas of .83 to .90 (Johnson 2014, Table
1: N .90, E .89, O .83, A .87, C .90). Cèrcol does not inherit the Furnham
critique of the underlying scales.

**Better: what is claimed.** Belbin's model was sold as a predictor of team
performance. SCIENCE.md explicitly disclaims that: "That roles predict team
performance" is in the "does not claim" list.

**No better: the discriminant-validity problem.** Aritzeta et al.'s finding,
"strong associations between some team roles […] weak discriminant validity"
is structurally guaranteed for Cèrcol, not merely likely. Twelve centroids
in a five-dimensional space with a minimum pairwise separation of 1.414 z
(Falcon and Badger) cannot be discriminated by a five-dimensional profile
measured with domain reliabilities near .88. Section 6 quantifies this: the
median respondent's top role carries 20% probability and 43.5% of
respondents would receive a different animal on a retest. Cèrcol's roles are
not *less* confusable than Belbin's; they are more numerous in a
lower-dimensional space.

**No better: adverse impact.** Anderson & Sleap's gender finding transfers
directly and has not been checked. Sex differences in Big Five domain means
are well documented (largest on Agreeableness and Neuroticism), and Cèrcol
z-scores against a single sex-pooled prior. Bond appears at ±1.0 in eight of
the twelve centroids. A systematic sex difference in role assignment is the
expected consequence, not a risk. It is currently unmeasured.

### And Cèrcol's own Belbin article is miscited

`/blog/big-five-vs-disc-vs-belbin`, live in all six languages, contains:

## 4. Categorising continuous personality data

### The taxometric evidence: personality is not made of types

Meehl's taxometric programme was built precisely to answer "is this latent
variable a category or a dimension". Two syntheses settle the question for
normal-range personality.

**Haslam, N., Holland, E., & Kuppens, P. (2012). Categories versus dimensions
in personality and psychopathology: a quantitative review of taxometric
research. *Psychological Medicine, 42*(5), 903–920.**
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291711001966
**Route:** abstract read verbatim at PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21939592/

> "We have conducted a comprehensive review of published taxometric research
> that included 177 articles, 311 distinct findings and a combined sample of
> 533 377 participants. […] Although 38.9% of findings were taxonic, these
> findings were much less frequent in more recent and methodologically
> stronger studies […] **When these and other possible confounds were
> statistically controlled, the true prevalence of taxonic findings was
> estimated at 14%. The domains of normal personality, mood disorders,
> anxiety disorders, eating disorders, externalizing disorders, and
> personality disorders (PDs) other than schizotypal yielded little
> persuasive evidence of taxa.** […] This review indicates that most latent
> variables of interest to psychiatrists and personality and clinical
> psychologists are dimensional, and that many influential taxonic findings
> of early taxometric research are likely to be spurious."

**Haslam, N., McGrath, M. J., Viechtbauer, W., & Kuppens, P. (2020).
Dimensions over categories: a meta-analysis of taxometric research.
*Psychological Medicine, 50*(9), 1418–1432.**
https://doi.org/10.1017/S003329172000183X
**Route:** abstract read verbatim via OpenAlex.

> "We report the first meta-analysis of taxometric research, examining 317
> findings drawn from 183 articles […] **Findings supporting dimensional
> models outnumbered those supporting taxonic models five to one.** […]
> **No content domain showed aggregate support for the taxonic model.** […]
> The meta-analytic findings support the conclusion that the great majority
> of psychological differences between people are latently continuous."

Directly on the Big Five domains: Longley, S. L., Miller, S. A.,
Broman-Fulks, J., Calamari, J. E., Holm-Denoma, J. M., & Meyers, K. (2017).
Taxometric analyses of higher-order personality domains. *Personality and
Individual Differences, 108*, 207–219.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2016.12.018, taxometric analyses of the five
domains in three samples (Ns = 857, 1280, 9935) across the BFI, NEO-PI-R and
Big Five Factor Markers, with dimensional latent structures supported in all
three. **Caveat on this one:** ScienceDirect returned 403 and the abstract is
absent from OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and PubMed; I have the sample sizes,
measures and conclusion from the ScienceDirect and PsycNet result summaries
but did not open the article. It should be cited only as supporting detail
behind Haslam et al., which I did read.

### Gerlach et al. (2018): what it does and does not support

**Gerlach, M., Farb, B., Revelle, W., & Nunes Amaral, L. A. (2018). A robust
data-driven approach identifies four personality types across four large data
sets. *Nature Human Behaviour, 2*(10), 735–742.**
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0419-z
**Route:** abstract read verbatim at PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31406291/
(article itself paywalled at Nature).

> "Here we develop an alternative approach to the identification of
> personality types, which we apply to four large data sets comprising more
> than 1.5 million participants. **We find robust evidence for at least four
> distinct personality types**, extending and refining previously suggested
> typologies. **We show that these types appear as a small subset of a much
> more numerous set of spurious solutions in typical clustering approaches,
> highlighting principal limitations in the blind application of unsupervised
> machine learning methods to the analysis of big data.**"

The four types are named *average*, *reserved*, *role model* and
*self-centred* (Northwestern's own release,
https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2018/september/are-you-average-reserved-self-centered-or-a-role-model).

Three things this does for Cèrcol, and one it does not.

**It does not support twelve.** It supports four, in 1.5 million people, and
the whole methodological point of the paper is that most clustering solutions
you can find in this data are spurious. A twelve-way partition asserted a
priori on a few hundred respondents is on the wrong side of that sentence.

**Its types are density peaks, not a partition.** They are locations where
respondents pile up more than a null model predicts. Cèrcol's centroids are a
partition: every respondent is assigned to exactly one of twelve, whatever
the density.

**Most people are not in any type.** Katahira, K., Kunisato, Y., Yamashita,
Y., & Suzuki, S. (2020). Commentary: A robust data-driven approach identifies
four personality types across four large data sets. *Frontiers in Big Data,
3*, 8. https://doi.org/10.3389/fdata.2020.00008 (open access, read in full at
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7931870/):

> "only less than half (42%) of the respondents was classified into four
> meaningful clusters"

and the commentary's substantive charge:

> "a skewed distribution with no cluster structures in personality space can
> erroneously lead to the seemingly meaningful clusters"

with their own simulation showing "only 45.5% of the samples […] are
classified into one of the three 'meaningful clusters'".

**And the finding is contested in the same journal.** Freudenstein, J.-P.,
Strauch, C., Mussel, P., & Ziegler, M. (2019). Four personality types may be
neither robust nor exhaustive. *Nature Human Behaviour, 3*(10), 1045–1046.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-019-0721-4, with a reply from the original
authors at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-019-0722-3. (Both verified via
Crossref; both paywalled, and I read only the titles and the Katahira et al.
characterisation of the Freudenstein argument, not the exchange itself.)

### Recommended replacement text

Add to `SCIENCE.md`, as a new subsection under "System structure":

> ### What the taxometric evidence says about role assignment
>
> Normal-range personality is latently continuous. Haslam, Holland & Kuppens
> (2012) reviewed 311 taxometric findings across 533,377 participants and,
> after controlling for methodological confounds, estimated the true
> prevalence of categorical findings at 14%, with "the domains of normal
> personality […] yield[ing] little persuasive evidence of taxa". The 2020
> meta-analysis by the same group found dimensional results outnumbering
> taxonic ones five to one, with "no content domain show[ing] aggregate
> support for the taxonic model".
>
> The strongest evidence for anything like personality types is Gerlach et
> al. (2018), which found four density clusters across 1.5 million
> respondents. It supports four, not twelve; its types are density peaks
> rather than a partition; and only 42% of respondents in one of its data
> sets fell into any of them (Katahira et al., 2020). Its own abstract warns
> that such types "appear as a small subset of a much more numerous set of
> spurious solutions in typical clustering approaches".
>
> Cèrcol therefore does not claim its twelve roles are latent categories, and
> the validation plan's k-means milestone at N≥300 should be read with this
> literature in mind: if the data are continuous, k-means will return twelve
> centroids regardless, because that is what k-means does. The milestone can
> tell us whether the *authored* centroids sit where the density is. It
> cannot tell us whether the categories exist.

## 5. Nearest-centroid classification: what it costs Cèrcol

### The instability is not hypothetical and it is larger than MBTI's

`/blog/mbti-vs-big-five-which-should-your-team-use` criticises the MBTI in
these terms:

> "Here is the core empirical problem with MBTI: when people retake it a few
> weeks later, a large share of them receive a different four-letter type. A
> widely cited review found that roughly **50 % of participants receive a
> different MBTI type when retested just five weeks later**. This happens
> because **the instrument forces continuous traits into binary categories**:
> someone who scores right on the E/I borderline will tip to one side or the
> other depending on how they are feeling that week."

That mechanism is exactly Cèrcol's nearest-centroid rule. Independent
confirmation that this is the standard criticism, from the only systematic
review and meta-analysis of the MBTI: Randall, K., Isaacson, M., & Ciro, C.
(2017). Validity and reliability of the Myers-Briggs Personality Type
Indicator: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Best Practices
in Health Professions Diversity, 10*(1), 1–27 (read in full, open copy at
https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/2017-randall.pdf), p. 4:

> "Most criticisms of the MBTI relate to the dichotomous nature of the
> instrument, **its translation of continuous scale scores into nominal
> categories of preference**, and whether it reflects the theory on which it
> is based."

### Simulation against Cèrcol's actual centroids

Method: draw a true five-dimensional z-profile; add independent measurement
error under the classical true-score model with error variance (1 − reliability)
per domain; assign by minimum Euclidean distance to the twelve centroids in
`src/utils/role-scoring.js`; repeat as a second independent administration;
count role changes. N = 400,000 per condition. Script:
`scratchpad/papers/stability.py`.

Reliabilities are the published ones, not guesses:
- IPIP-NEO-120 domain alphas, Johnson (2014) Table 1, read from the author's
  own copy at
  https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/1/163537/files/2023/07/IPIPNEO120.pdf
 , N .90, E .89, O .83, A .87, C .90.
- TIPI six-week test-retest, Gosling, Rentfrow & Swann (2003) Table 4, N = 180,
  read from the author's copy at
  https://gosling.psy.utexas.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/JRP-03-tipi.pdf
 , E .77, A .71, C .76, ES .70, O .62, mean .72. The table note reads: "TIPI
  retest interval was approximately 6 weeks."

| Instrument | Reliability source | Role changes on retest |
|---|---|---|
| Full Moon (IPIP-NEO-120) | Johnson 2014 domain alphas | **43.5%** |
| First Quarter (IPIP-NEO-60) | .85 assumed | 46.2% |
| New Moon (TIPI) | Gosling 2003 six-week retest | **57.2%** |

Alpha is an upper bound on retest stability, so 43.5% is the optimistic
figure for Full Moon. New Moon's 57.2% is worse than the MBTI number the blog
uses to condemn the MBTI, and New Moon is the free front door to the product.

Two further numbers from the same run, standard normal profile space:

- **Median top-role probability: 20.0%.** Half of all respondents have a
  primary role their own probability map gives less than 20%, against the
  8.3% floor of a uniform guess.
- Role frequencies are uneven by construction, ranging 4.7% (Tortoise) to
  11.3% (Octopus), a 2.4:1 ratio, before any real-population skew. Tortoise's
  centroid is 1.908 z from the origin, the furthest of the twelve, and
  Dolphin's is 1.500, the nearest; a distance-based rule gives the nearer
  centroid a larger catchment.
- Minimum pairwise centroid separation is 1.414 z (Falcon and Badger), which
  is less than the typical distance a respondent moves between two
  administrations of the TIPI.

### What follows

The `arc` mechanism (roles above 15% probability) already exists and is the
right instinct: the median respondent's arc is not a footnote, it is the
result. But `computeRole` returns a single `role` field, the report leads with
one animal, and the product page is `/role/dolphin`. The architecture treats
the point estimate as the answer and the uncertainty as decoration, which is
the reverse of what the numbers support.

### Recommended text

Add to `SCIENCE.md` under "Scoring pipeline v2":

> ### Stability of the assignment
>
> Nearest-centroid assignment converts a continuous profile into one of twelve
> labels, and the label is much less stable than the profile that produced it.
> Simulating two independent administrations under the classical true-score
> model, with published domain reliabilities and the centroids in
> `role-scoring.js`, the assigned role changes for 43.5% of respondents on the
> IPIP-NEO-120 (Johnson 2014 domain alphas: N .90, E .89, O .83, A .87, C .90)
> and 57.2% on the TIPI (Gosling et al. 2003 six-week retest: mean r = .72).
> The median respondent's top role carries 20% probability.
>
> This is not a defect of the implementation. It is what happens when twelve
> centroids with a minimum separation of 1.414 z partition a five-dimensional
> continuum. It is the same mechanism Cèrcol criticises in the MBTI, and the
> New Moon number is worse than the MBTI's. The dimension scores are the
> stable measurement; the role is a reading of them. The report should be
> built so that a user who retakes the instrument and gets a different animal
> has been told in advance that this is expected.

## 6. The honest framing

### Is Cèrcol doing the thing it criticises?

On the specific charge in its own MBTI article, forcing continuous traits
into discrete categories, with the resulting retest instability, **yes, and
more so**. 57.2% versus the 50% it cites against the MBTI.

On three other charges it is genuinely different, and the documentation
should say which is which rather than blur them:

| Charge Cèrcol makes against MBTI | Applies to Cèrcol? |
|---|---|
| Continuous traits forced into categories | **Yes**, and measurably worse on New Moon |
| Types unstable on retest | **Yes**, 43.5% to 57.2% |
| Underlying measurement unvalidated | **No**. IPIP-NEO domain alphas .83–.90 |
| Claims to predict job performance | **No**. Explicitly disclaimed in SCIENCE.md |
| Proprietary, unauditable | **No**. Public domain items, open source |

The difference that matters is that Cèrcol's *measurement* is sound and its
*labels* are authored. MBTI's problem is that both are weak. That is a real
distinction and Cèrcol is entitled to it, but only if it stops describing the
labels as though they inherited the measurement's standing.

### Where the documentation currently overclaims

Three places, in descending order of seriousness.

**(a) The blog article about the roles describes a construction that is not
the one in the code.** `/blog/the-12-cercol-team-roles-explained`:

> "The AB5C defines a circumplex for each of the ten possible pairs of
> factors. Cèrcol anchors its 12 roles in two of them:
> - The **social balance** plane (Presence × Bond, i.e., Extraversion ×
>   Agreeableness) […]
> - The **cognitive balance** plane (Vision × Discipline, i.e., Openness ×
>   Conscientiousness) […]"

There is no Vision × Discipline plane in `role-scoring.js`. Discipline never
takes a defining ±1.0 value in any centroid; it carries offsets only. The
actual construction uses **three** planes: P×B, P×V and B×V. The article's
own table one screen later lists the correct profiles (Eagle as "Presence+,
Vision+"), so the article contradicts itself as well as the code. The
accompanying figure caption compounds it: "Four of them separate the roles
from each other."

**(b) SCIENCE.md's "does not claim" list is good but incomplete.** It
disclaims prediction, universality, and centroid correctness. It does not
disclaim the thing most readers will assume: that the roles are a scientific
taxonomy rather than an editorial one.

**(c) The MBTI article's own sourcing is loose.** Two figures in the same
article disagree (a "50%" stat card and a blockquote saying "As many as
three-quarters"), the blockquote is attributed only to "multiple independent
meta-analyses" with no citation, and the "r < 0.20" stat card has no source.
Pittenger, D. J. (1993). The utility of the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator.
*Review of Educational Research, 63*(4), 467–488,
https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543063004467, is the actual source of the
standard critique, its abstract concludes "there is insufficient evidence to
support the tenets of and claims about the utility of the test", and Randall
et al. (2017) is the only systematic review. Neither is cited.

### The text that says it honestly

For `SCIENCE.md`, replacing the "What this system does not claim" / "What this
system does claim" block:

> ### What the twelve roles are, and are not
>
> The measurement and the roles are two different things with two different
> standings, and conflating them is the main way a system like this misleads
> people.
>
> **The measurement is published science.** OCEAN is the most replicated
> structure in personality psychology. The items are public-domain IPIP. The
> domain reliabilities are published: .83 to .90 on the 120-item inventory
> (Johnson, 2014), a mean six-week retest of .72 on the ten-item one (Gosling
> et al., 2003). Anyone can audit the items and rerun the scoring.
>
> **The twelve roles are an authored layer on top of it.** The geometry comes
> from the AB5C: three of its ten circumplex planes, four corners each. The
> coordinates within those planes, the Discipline and Depth offsets, the
> twelve names and every word of the twelve descriptions are Cèrcol's. No
> published source assigns a person to a Dolphin or a Badger, because no
> published source contains those categories. The AB5C is a taxonomy of trait
> adjectives, and its authors decline the extension to types: "We do not wish
> to imply that types should fit into one of the boxes of the AB5C taxonomy"
> (Hofstee et al., 1992, p. 161).
>
> **The roles are also less stable than the profile.** Assigning by nearest
> centroid changes the assigned role for 43.5% of respondents between two
> administrations of the 120-item instrument, and 57.2% of the ten-item one.
> The median respondent's top role carries 20% probability. Personality
> itself is latently continuous: the largest review of taxometric research
> puts the true prevalence of categorical findings in normal personality near
> 14% (Haslam, Holland & Kuppens, 2012), and the strongest evidence for
> anything like types found four, not twelve, in 1.5 million people, with only
> 42% of respondents falling into any of them (Gerlach et al., 2018; Katahira
> et al., 2020).
>
> **So the honest statement of what a role is:** a name for the region of
> profile space you are currently nearest to, chosen because a name is easier
> to talk about in a team than five numbers. It is a lens, not a finding. If
> you retake the instrument and get a different animal, nothing has gone
> wrong and nothing about you has changed; you were near a boundary, which
> half of everyone is. The five dimension scores are the measurement. The
> animal is how Cèrcol talks about it.
>
> This is also why Cèrcol criticises the MBTI for turning continuous scores
> into discrete types while doing a version of the same thing. The difference
> is not that Cèrcol has solved the problem. It is that Cèrcol's measurement
> is validated and open where the MBTI's is not, and that Cèrcol says out
> loud what its labels are.

For `PRODUCT.md`, adding to "Team balance framing":

> The report must never present the role as a result the user could get wrong
> or right, or as a stable property. Alongside the primary role, always show
> the arc and the primary role's probability. Where the top probability is
> below roughly 25%, which is the majority of users, the copy should lead
> with the arc rather than the single animal. A user who retakes an
> instrument and gets a different animal should find that outcome already
> explained in the report they read the first time.

# Bonus

**1. The AB5C independently supports the "rare profiles" claim, but as a claim
about words, not people.** SCIENCE.md justifies Fox and Octopus being rare by
appeal to positive intercorrelations among E, A and O. Hofstee et al. offer a
different and stronger version, p. 155:

> "most of the configurations depicted in Figure 1 have a southwest versus
> northeast orientation, meaning that blends between equally valenced factor
> poles are more likely to occur; the only exception is the I × III
> configuration."

That is a lexical density finding: mixed-valence blends are sparsely populated
in the trait lexicon. It explains why Falcon, Octopus, Bear and Fox sit in
near-empty AB5C cells. It is a better citation than the unsourced correlation
figure, provided the claim is stated as being about descriptors.

**2. Bipolarity is weaker than the centroid table assumes.** Hofstee et al.,
p. 154:

> "Some 40% of the negations are not in the segment directly opposite that of
> their root. For example, *aggressive* is I+II+, but *unaggressive* is
> I−II+ (the difference is subtle; *aggressive* is slightly II−);
> *conscientious* is III+II+, but *unconscientious* is III−V−; *intellectual*
> is V+IV+, but their negations are purely V−."

Cèrcol's construction assumes each role's opposite pole is its geometric
reflection: Dolphin at (+1,+1) and Owl at (−1,−1). The AB5C data say that
holds for about 60% of the lexicon. Worth a line in the limitations.

**3. The witness corpus's cross-loading note has a source it does not cite.**
SCIENCE.md's Witness section says "The AB5C circumplex predicts that some
behavioural markers load on more than one factor (e.g. *assertive* on both E+
and A−)". That exact example is in the paper, p. 154: "*Assertive* has a II−,
III+, IV+, V+ pattern, whereas the opposite signs apply to *talkative*." Note
that the paper's finding is broader and more awkward than the current text:
*assertive* loads on four secondary factors, not one, and the paper's central
methodological complaint (p. 147) is that "the practice of neglecting
secondary loadings in simple-structure models may be deceptive". Absorbing
cross-loadings "as measurement noise" is exactly the practice the AB5C exists
to criticise. This is worth stating in the limitations rather than as an
aside.

**4. A cheap and immediate check on adverse impact.** Anderson & Sleap's
gender finding transfers structurally to Cèrcol (Bond appears at ±1.0 in eight
of twelve centroids; Big Five sex differences are largest on Agreeableness).
Cèrcol already stores results with language and instrument; if sex is
collected, a crosstab of assigned role by sex at current N would take an hour
and would either close the concern or surface it before anyone uses the
product for team composition. If sex is not collected, that is itself the
finding, and it should be stated in SCIENCE.md that adverse impact is
unmeasurable with the current data.
